How AI fits my process

workflow v2.6
01 / SKETCH

Direct, don't delegate

Every project starts analog — thumbnails, type studies, data-model sketches. AI never decides what to make; it widens the space of how.

02 / PROTOTYPE

Prompt-driven variants

I describe states and constraints in precise language and generate throwaway variants in minutes. Cheap exploration, expensive taste.

03 / BUILD

Verify everything

AI-suggested code is treated like a junior teammate's PR: read it, type-check it, test it, rewrite it when it's wrong. I own every line I ship.

04 / SHIP

Document the loop

Each case study notes exactly where AI helped and where it didn't. The workflow is part of the work — and it compounds with every project.

About

the artist's statement

I spent years as a freelance digital illustrator — commissions, print runs, tattoo and character design — before finishing a computer science degree at the University of Latvia. That order matters: I learned to see before I learned to build, and it shows in how I write interfaces.

Design engineering is the job where both halves get used at once. I care about type scales the way I care about type systems, and I think the best UI work happens when one person can carry an idea from Figma frame to production component without a handoff in between.

Currently in Riga, relocating toward Barcelona, where my family is — learning Spanish on the way. When I'm not shipping, I'm drawing, cooking Vietnamese food, or rearranging a shelf of Sylvanian Families with unreasonable precision.
